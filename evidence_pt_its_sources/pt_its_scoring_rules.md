# Public Transport Supply and Signal/ITS Scoring Rules

## Public transport supply score (0–3)

- **0 = No clear formal urban public transport evidence.**
- **1 = Basic public transport evidence** such as bus, minibus, songthaew, city bus, or local public transport, but no mature rapid-transit backbone.
- **2 = One rapid-transit mode or major formal bus/BRT modernization project**, including operational metro/BRT/LRT or a large formally funded city bus reform under implementation.
- **3 = Mature integrated public transport system**, usually a multimodal network with metro/LRT/MRT/BRT/bus or an extensive metropolitan public transport system.

## Signal / ITS score (0–3)

- **0 = No public city-level evidence located** for coordinated signal control, intelligent traffic management, or a traffic control centre.
- **1 = Basic traffic-management or smart-mobility evidence**, such as CCTV, route monitoring, smart-card bus systems, or a national ITS context without clear city-level signal coordination.
- **2 = Partial coordinated ITS or corridor/intersection-level smart signal evidence**, including ATCS, signal priority, or traffic-control projects.
- **3 = Advanced citywide or metropolitan ITS**, including traffic management centre, adaptive signal control, large camera networks, or centrally managed signalised intersections.

## Use in the paper

These scores are **proxy variables**, not direct percentages. They should be described as ordinal evidence-based scores because comparable city-level percentages of coordinated signal systems and public-transport supply are not available for all 22 cities.
